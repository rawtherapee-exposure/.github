## Core RawTherapee Features

- **Raw and JPEG:** Open photos from your camera or phone and adjust exposure, white balance, and crop.
- **The Negative Stays:** Edits sit beside the raw file. Exporting a JPEG does not overwrite the capture.
- **One Frame First:** A single picture shows whether the look is right before you copy it onto a whole folder.
- **A Normal Viewer Check:** The JPEG you export is what other people will see. Judge that file, not only the develop preview.

---

## About RawTherapee

RawTherapee is a photo developer for pictures you took. You import a folder from a card or a disk, change the light and the crop, and write a JPEG or similar file when the frame looks right. It does not include a stock library, and it does not remove a watermark from an image you do not own.

The raw file is the one you can return to. If the export is too dark or too small, you change the settings and export again. You do not need to reshoot unless the capture itself was unusable. Keep the card until the computer copy has opened.

Start with one shoot, not every picture you have ever taken. A catalog of thousands of frames is harder to trust until you know where RawTherapee expects the files to live. If you move the folder later, the session can lose the pictures even though they are still on the disk.

## Tool Benefits

- **A Reversible Look:** The raw remains, so a bad export is only a bad JPEG.
- **A Clear Delivery File:** The JPEG is the picture you attach or print.
- **A Small First Import:** One folder teaches the window before a large archive does.

## Getting Started with RawTherapee

Prerequisites: A folder of your own photos and room for JPEG exports.

1. **Open One Folder:** Import or browse the shoot you just copied off the card.
2. **Adjust One Frame:** Set exposure and crop, then look at it large enough to judge.
3. **Export a JPEG:** Use a new name. Do not replace the raw.
4. **View the JPEG:** Open it outside RawTherapee. Keep the raw until that picture is the one you want.

## Professional & Personal Use Cases

- **A Shoot Handoff:** Develop the frames you will actually deliver and leave the rest as raws.
- **A Print or a Page:** Export the size the page needs instead of sending the raw.
- **A Second Look:** Re-export from the raw if the first JPEG was too contrasty.
- **An Archive:** Store the raws and the JPEGs in folders you can explain a year later.
